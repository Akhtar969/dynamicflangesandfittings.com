TYPES OF MS FLANGES

MS Flange Types We Manufacture

As an MS flanges manufacturer in Mumbai, we make five main types. They differ mainly in how they attach to the pipe. Pick the type by pressure, temperature and how the joint is assembled, then request a quotation.

Closes the end of a line

Blind Flanges

A blind flange is a solid disc with no bore. It is bolted to a mating flange to close the end of a pipe, nozzle or vessel opening.

Connection
Bolted to a mating flange
Best for
Isolating a line, tank and vessel openings, future expansion
Request quote
For higher pressure and temperature

Welding Neck Flanges

A welding neck flange has a long tapered hub that is butt-welded to the pipe. The hub carries stress smoothly, so the joint stays strong and leak-tight.

Connection
Butt weld to the pipe
Best for
Higher pressure and temperature lines, cyclic loads
Request quote
Easy to fit and align

Slip-On Flanges

A slip-on flange slips over the pipe and is fillet-welded inside and outside. It is easy to align and cheaper than a weld neck flange.

Connection
Fillet weld, inside and outside
Best for
General piping, water and utility lines

Flat plate, cut and drilled

Plate Flanges

A plate flange is a flat plate cut to size, bored and drilled to your specification. It suits low-pressure lines and custom drilling.

PRESSURE CLASSES

Class 150, 300 and 600 Flanges Explained

Class is the pressure rating group of a flange. A higher class means a thicker, heavier flange with a larger bolt circle and more or larger bolts. For carbon steel at ambient temperature, Class 150 is about 19.6 bar, Class 300 about 51.1 bar and Class 600 about 102.1 bar.

Class Approx. working pressure* Flange build Typical use
Class 150 About 19.6 bar Lightest and thinnest, 4 to 12 bolts up to 12″ Water, air and general utility lines
Class 300 About 51.1 bar Thicker with a wider bolt circle and larger holes Process lines and moderate-pressure steam
Class 600 About 102.1 bar Thickest and heaviest, up to 20 bolts at 12″ High-pressure process lines

*Approximate rating at ambient temperature for carbon steel (material group 1.1). Actual rating falls as temperature rises and depends on the material grade. Confirm with your design engineer.

MANUFACTURING PROCESS

How MS Flanges Are Manufactured at Our Mumbai Factory

As an MS flanges manufacturer, we make flanges in seven steps: material verification, blank cutting, machining, drilling, finishing, inspection, and packing and dispatch. Every flange is checked against the drawing or class table before it leaves.

  1. Material selection and verificationMS plate or bar is selected to the order and verified. MTC can be requested for the lot.
  2. Cutting the blankThe blank is cut to the flange outside diameter and bore size from plate or bar.
  3. MachiningOutside diameter, bore, thickness and face are machined. Weld neck hubs are formed, and raised face or flat face is finished as specified.
  4. Drilling bolt holesHoles are drilled on the bolt circle, with the number and size set by the class or table.
  5. FinishingDeburring and chamfering, then plain, oiled or coated as ordered.
  6. InspectionDimensions (OD, thickness, PCD, bolt holes, bore) and surface are checked against your drawing or standard.
  7. Marking, packing and dispatchFlanges are marked, packed to avoid damage, and dispatched with documents where available.

How the process changes by class

Class 150 Lightest blank, shortest machining time 4 to 12 holes up to 12″ OD, bolt circle and hole count
Class 300 Thicker blank and more machining Larger holes and more holes on bigger sizes Thickness and bolt circle accuracy
Class 600 Thickest, heaviest blank and longest machining Up to 20 large holes at 12″ Thickness, hole size and hub dimensions

QUESTIONS ANSWERED

MS Flanges FAQs: Types, Classes and Dimensions

Quick answers from an MS flanges manufacturer in Mumbai. Need more? Contact us.

What is an MS flange?

An MS (mild steel) flange is a ring-shaped fitting that joins pipes, valves and equipment with bolts. It lets a piping system be assembled and dismantled for inspection or maintenance. MS flanges suit general industrial piping, water lines and structural work.

What types of MS flanges do you manufacture?

We manufacture blind, welding neck, slip-on, plate and E table flanges. Other types such as socket weld, lap joint, orifice, forged and male-female flanges can be quoted on enquiry. Each type differs in how it attaches to the pipe.

What does flange class 150, 300 and 600 mean?

Class is the pressure rating group of a flange. Higher class means a thicker, heavier flange with a larger bolt circle and more or larger bolts. For carbon steel at ambient temperature, Class 150 is about 19.6 bar, Class 300 about 51.1 bar and Class 600 about 102.1 bar.

What is the difference between Class 150, 300 and 600 flanges?

The main differences are thickness, outside diameter, bolt circle and bolt holes. For a 4 inch flange, outside diameter is 228.6 mm for Class 150, 254.0 mm for Class 300 and 273.1 mm for Class 600, and thickness rises from 23.9 mm to 31.8 mm to 38.1 mm.

What are the dimensions of a 4 inch Class 150 flange?

A 4 inch (DN 100) Class 150 flange has an outside diameter of 228.6 mm, a minimum thickness of 23.9 mm, a bolt circle of 190.5 mm and 8 bolt holes of 19.1 mm. See the full Class 150 table on this page.

What size range of MS flanges do you make?

The dimension tables on this page cover NPS ½ to 12 inch (DN 15 to DN 300) for Class 150, 300 and 600. Other sizes, thicknesses and custom drilling can be quoted to your drawing. Send the size and class with your enquiry.

What is the difference between Table D and Table E flanges?

Table D and Table E are drilling and dimension tables from the British Standard BS 10. They differ in pressure rating, flange thickness and bolt pattern, so they are not interchangeable. Always state the table named on your drawing.

How are MS flanges manufactured?

MS flanges are made by selecting and verifying material, cutting the blank, machining the outside diameter, bore and face, drilling bolt holes on the bolt circle, finishing, and inspecting dimensions before dispatch. Thicker Class 300 and 600 flanges need more machining and tighter checks.

What is the difference between raised face and flat face flanges?

A raised face flange has a raised ring around the bore that concentrates gasket load and is common on Class 150 and 300. A flat face flange has a fully flat sealing surface and is used where the mating flange is flat. State the face type in your enquiry.

What do you need to quote MS flanges?

Send the flange type, nominal size, class or table (such as Class 150 or Table E), face type, material, standard or drawing, and quantity. Add MTC if you need test certificates. We reply with a quotation.

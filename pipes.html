SCHEDULES AND CLASSES

Sch 10, 40, 80, 160 and IS 1239 Light, Medium, Heavy Pipes

Schedule is a number that sets pipe wall thickness. A higher schedule means a thicker wall and higher pressure capacity. IS 1239 pipes use light, medium and heavy classes instead of schedules. Outside diameter stays the same for a given nominal size.

Grade Wall thickness Typical use
Sch 10 Thin wall (3.05 mm at 4″) Low-pressure water and air
Sch 40 (STD) Standard wall (6.02 mm at 4″) General industrial piping
Sch 80 (XS) Heavy wall (8.56 mm at 4″) Higher-pressure process and steam lines
Sch 160 Very heavy wall (13.49 mm at 4″) High-pressure service
IS 1239 Medium 4.5 mm at 100 mm bore Water, gas and general piping
IS 1239 Heavy 5.4 mm at 100 mm bore Heavier-duty water and gas piping

Other schedules such as Sch 20, 30, STD, XS and XXS can be quoted on enquiry.

HOW PIPES ARE MADE

How ERW and Seamless MS Pipes Are Made

ERW pipe is formed from steel coil and welded along a seam. Seamless pipe is pierced and rolled from a solid billet. Both are sized, cut to length and tested by the pipe mill before supply.

ERW pipe process

  1. Steel coil is uncoiled and the edges are prepared.
  2. The strip is formed into a round tube.
  3. The seam is welded by electric resistance.
  4. The weld is trimmed and heat treated.
  5. The tube is sized, straightened and cut to length.
  6. The pipe is tested and the ends are finished.

Seamless pipe process

  1. A solid steel billet is heated.
  2. The billet is pierced to form a hollow shell.
  3. The shell is rolled to the pipe size and wall.
  4. The pipe is sized and straightened.
  5. It is cut to length and heat treated as required.
  6. The pipe is tested and the ends are finished.

How schedule changes pipe making

Schedule Wall at 4″ Effect on making and testing
Sch 10 3.05 mm Thin wall; light forming, mostly ERW
Sch 40 (STD) 6.02 mm Common wall; ERW or seamless
Sch 80 (XS) 8.56 mm Heavier forming or rolling; often seamless; higher test pressure
Sch 160 13.49 mm Heavy wall; usually seamless; heavy rolling and strict wall checks

QUESTIONS ANSWERED

MS Pipes FAQs: Types, Schedules and Dimensions

Quick answers from an MS pipes supplier in Mumbai. Need more? Contact us.

What types of MS pipes do you supply?

We supply ERW, seamless, black and galvanised (GI) MS pipes for industrial piping. MS round pipes, and square and rectangular pipes, can be quoted on enquiry. Sizes, schedule and length are confirmed in the quotation.

What is the difference between ERW and seamless pipes?

ERW pipe is made from coil welded along a seam and costs less, so it suits general piping. Seamless pipe is rolled from a solid billet with no seam and handles higher pressure and temperature. Choose by pressure, temperature and fluid.

What is a pipe schedule?

Schedule is a number that sets the wall thickness of a pipe for a given nominal size. A higher schedule means a thicker wall and higher pressure capacity. Outside diameter stays the same for a nominal size, and only the inside diameter changes.

What is the wall thickness and weight of a 4 inch Sch 40 pipe?

A 4 inch (DN 100) Sch 40 pipe has an outside diameter of 114.3 mm, a wall thickness of 6.02 mm and a weight of about 16.07 kg per metre. Sch 80 has an 8.56 mm wall and weighs about 22.3 kg per metre.

What is the difference between black and galvanised pipe?

Black pipe is plain MS pipe with a dark mill finish. Galvanised (GI) pipe is coated with zinc for added corrosion resistance and is common on water lines and outdoor work. Black pipe costs less and is often painted.

What are IS 1239 light, medium and heavy pipes?

IS 1239 is the Indian Standard for steel tubes used for water, gas and general piping, and it groups pipes into light, medium and heavy classes by wall thickness. A 100 mm bore medium pipe has a 4.5 mm wall and a heavy pipe has a 5.4 mm wall.

How do pipe sizes like NPS, DN and outside diameter relate?

NPS (inch) and DN (mm) are nominal sizes, not exact measurements. A 4 inch NPS pipe is DN 100 and has an actual outside diameter of 114.3 mm. Always state nominal size and schedule, or outside diameter and wall thickness.

How are ERW and seamless pipes made?

ERW pipe: steel coil is formed into a tube, welded along the seam, sized, cut and tested. Seamless pipe: a heated billet is pierced, rolled to size and wall, then cut and tested. Thicker schedules need heavier rolling or forming and more testing.
